## Where AI Was Wrong or Misleading

### Case 1: Localization splitting faults
**Problem:** AI's initial DFS continued recursing into dark subtrees after finding a fault boundary. This meant one snapped wire produced one ticket for each dark pole (40 tickets instead of 1).  
**How I caught it:** Ran a mental simulation: one span fault, DT has 10 downstream poles. Traced the recursion on paper. Found it emitted 10 boundary events.  
**Fix:** Added the early return after collecting `collectDarkDescendants` — once a boundary is found, stop recursing that branch entirely.

### Case 2: Timestamp-based dedup was wrong
**Problem:** AI suggested deduplicating telemetry by `(device_id, ts)`. The assignment explicitly says device timestamps can skew ±90s, meaning two real different events might have the same timestamp, and one real event might arrive twice with different server-received times.  
**How I caught it:** Re-read the spec: "seq is your one reliable tool for ordering and deduplication within a device."  
**Fix:** Dedup by `(device_id, seq)` via the `device_seq` table, not by timestamp.

### Case 3: MST rooting
**Problem:** AI's first MST implementation (Kruskal's) built the minimum spanning tree but had no concept of a root node. When I asked it to "root at the DT location," it added the DT as just another node with equal weight, which sometimes got placed as a leaf.  
**How I caught it:** Checked generated test trees — several had the DT at the end of a chain instead of the root.  
**Fix:** Switched to Prim's algorithm, explicitly seeding with the DT node first and only adding poles to the MST from there.

## Best Prompts / Sessions

**Most useful prompt:**
> "The power network is a radial tree. Sensors are on nodes (poles). When a span (edge) fails, all downstream nodes go dark. Write a DFS function that finds the live/dark boundary — the last live node before the first dark node — and returns one boundary object per disconnected dark subtree. Do NOT recurse into dark subtrees after finding a boundary."

The explicit "do NOT recurse" constraint was what fixed Case 1 above. AI needs very precise constraints on control flow for recursive algorithms.

**Second most useful prompt:**
> "I have pole devices with timestamps that skew ±90s and messages that can arrive up to 6 hours late. The spec says `seq` is monotonic per device and resets on `boot`. Design an idempotent UPSERT for pole_states that correctly handles: out-of-order arrival, duplicate messages, and a device that reboots (seq resets)."

This produced the correct UPSERT-with-GREATEST pattern. I verified it against 4 edge cases before committing.
